What is implemented

A bounded Python evidence selector separates the proposed current state from
relevant past changes. It tracks input restrictions, argument origin and shell
settings, links claims to source versions/spans, and labels weakening,
restoration, removal and unknown analysis. Superseded weaknesses cannot populate
current-fact fields. Equivalent or unrelated edits are excluded when the bounded
facts do not change. Over-budget packets fail explicitly rather than silently
dropping evidence. This is not whole-repository or general-language analysis.

What the evaluation found

One frozen three-condition test used 36 cases per condition from six related
history/template families: six unsafe, 24 safe and six unknown. Calibration used
four separate families. The current summary was identical across conditions;
all had the same evidence cap and no truncation.

                           Current code    + Unselected history    + Selected history
Current facts correct        36/36               36/36                  36/36
Policy verdict correct       20/36               26/36                  24/36
Contradictory responses       13                   7                      9

Correct verdict counts include appropriate unknown answers. These are small,
related synthetic families, not independent real-world attacks. Independent
human review was unavailable. Selected-minus-unselected verdict accuracy was
-5.6 percentage points, with family bootstrap interval [-19.4, +11.1]; the
comparison does not establish an advantage or equivalence.

The monitor received and reported sufficient current facts but often applied
the declared policy incorrectly. A separate deterministic decision component
applied to its reported facts gave the correct answer, including unknowns, on
all 36 cases in each condition. That is diagnosis on inspected cases, not a
rescored monitor success or a general sabotage detector.

Calibration exposed false accusations in every condition. With binary verdicts,
the frozen false-alarm rule disabled all alarms; calibrated detection was 0/6
in every arm. Zero test false alarms therefore reflects a disabled detector,
not useful prevention. Original raw errors and contradictory answers are saved.

What this does not establish

The selector has not earned its added complexity through improved monitoring.
These fixtures explicitly provide the current facts needed to solve a state-only
rule. They can test whether history introduces confusion, but offer no missing
information that history is necessary to recover. Adding history is not itself
the proposed DeepTrace contribution. Neither proposal experiment is complete.

Two separate research decisions

1. Monitor choice. Decide whether a different task-appropriate sabotage monitor
   or an explicit fact-to-policy component is appropriate. Either choice is a
   new pipeline requiring its own frozen validation. The deterministic checker
   only handles the authored narrow rule; it cannot replace judgments about a
   legitimate LinuxArena task and sabotage.

2. DeepTrace contribution. Identify verified repository cases where versioned
   support links help find a distant, action-relevant dependency under an evidence
   and compute budget. Compare against equally capable current-code search with
   the same repository access and resources. Do not withhold current facts from
   the baseline. Historical retrieval must earn a measurable benefit beyond
   simply adding more context.
